# How much does boosting improve NYC home price predictions?

## Group
The Outliers: Kuangye, Tianlin, Jiayi.

## Changes since the proposal
We retained the research question and residential cohort. Following feedback, we added an audit of properties appearing in both training and test periods, reported validation performance across time, and completed a paired block-bootstrap dry run on validation data.

## Data
We loaded five NYC Department of Finance workbooks covering October 2025-September 2026: 82,576 sales and 21 columns. Restricting to one-, two-, and three-family dwellings removed 45,688 records. Excluding prices below $10,000 removed another 18,216, leaving 18,672 observations. We selected 11 source input fields describing location, building type, units, size, construction year, and sale date. The output is natural-log sale price.

## Split
We used three expanding-window validation splits, following the approved proposal feedback: train October-December, validate January-February; train through February, validate March-April; train through April, validate May-June. The development period contains 14,227 sales. July-September's 4,445 sales remain reserved for final performance evaluation. We did not shuffle observations. Imputation, category grouping, encoding, and scaling were fitted separately within each training window. The bootstrap seed was 5420.

## Baseline
We fitted ridge regression using log areas, property age and age squared, elapsed time, borough-area interactions, missingness indicators, and one-hot categorical features. We evaluated 12 penalties: 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000, 100,000, 1,000,000, 10,000,000, and 100,000,000. Minimizing mean validation log-price MAE across the three windows selected alpha=10.

## Result
Mean validation log-price MAE was 0.26585, with a 95% bootstrap interval of [0.25800, 0.27421]. Window MAEs were 0.25703, 0.26909, and 0.27143; their sample standard deviation was 0.00773. We drew 1,000 bootstrap samples of tax blocks within boroughs, preserving each sampled block's transactions across windows, and recomputed the equally weighted mean of the three MAEs. This interval conditions on the selected penalty and fitted models; it excludes tuning uncertainty and future market shifts. Test performance has not been evaluated.

## Issues found
The residential categories included 17,196 nonpositive-price records. Our price screen does not establish that retained sales reflect market value. Snapshot attributes may reflect later updates. Among 4,423 distinct test properties, 190 had an earlier retained training-period sale, accounting for 210 test transactions. We checked this overlap using parcel identifiers, without test predictions. Manhattan's small sample may limit borough-level conclusions.
